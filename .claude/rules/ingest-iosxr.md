# `rsim-ingest`, IOS-XR

The IOS-XR module is in `iosxr/`: `iosxr/table.rs` classifies the lines of the running config, with the `end-policy` and `end-set` lines in the state of their policy or set, `iosxr/mask.rs` masks the secrets, `iosxr/config.rs` reads the interfaces, the VRFs, the IPv4 ACLs with the ACL entry reader of NX-OS and the IPv4 groups of `router hsrp`, `iosxr/state.rs` parses the text sections `interfaces`, `vrfs`, `rib`, `arp`, `lldp`, `cdp` and `hsrp` through the templates of `rsim-textfsm` after it removes the time line, with the line range of each record as its provenance, `iosxr/lower.rs` lowers a router with the ACL and QoS functions of `nxos/lower.rs` and gives its summary for the link join, and `iosxr/profile.rs` holds the vendor profile, the interface types for the short names, the words of the sections and the HSRP state words. `Template::parse_traced` of `rsim-textfsm` gives the input lines of each record and the lines that no record holds. A route table line that the template does not read makes the lookup of its prefix unknown; a path line takes the prefix of the route line above it, and a line with no prefix makes each lookup of its VRF unknown. A backup path `(!)` is not a path of the lookup. A path with no interface resolves through `resolve.rs`, and a next hop in another VRF is unknown. An unreadable ACL entry with a sequence number stays at its place with match bounds. `show vrf all detail` is a cross-check of the `vrf` lines; a difference gives a note.

`iosxr/bgp_config.rs` lowers the BGP config for the BGP calculation: the process of the default VRF and of each `vrf` stanza with `bgp router-id`, `bgp cluster-id`, `bgp bestpath med always`, `med missing-as-worst`, `compare-routerid` and `as-path multipath-relax`, `bgp default local-preference`, `maximum-paths ebgp` and `ibgp`, `network` with an optional `route-policy`, `aggregate-address` with `summary-only` and `as-set`, and the neighbours. A VRF with no `bgp router-id` line has no process and a gap. The settings of a neighbour in a unicast family come from its own address-family lines (`route-policy <name> in` and `out`, `route-reflector-client`, `next-hop-self`, `send-community-ebgp`, `send-extended-community-ebgp` and `remove-private-AS`, each `inheritance-disable` form), then from the af-group that the family uses, then from the address family of its neighbor-group and its af-group. A session to an iBGP or a confederation peer sends the communities. The route policies are read from their lines in order: `if`, `elseif`, `else` and `endif`, `pass`, `drop`, `done`, `apply`, the `set` actions, `prepend as-path` and `delete community in`, with conditions of `and`, `or`, `not` and parentheses over `destination in`, `next-hop in`, `community matches-any` and `matches-every`, `as-path in`, `neighbor-is`, `originates-from`, `passes-through` and `length`, `local-preference`, `med`, `origin is` and `tag`, with a named or an inline set. A condition that is not read is an unknown term, a statement that is not read is an unknown statement, a policy with parameters is one unknown statement, and a reference to a policy with parameters is a gap. The prefix sets, the community sets with values, wildcards, ranges and `ios-regex`, and the AS-path sets are lists of permit lines. A prefix set with an element that is not read is left out. Each `import` line of a unicast family of a VRF is a gap. `iosxr/bgp_config::collected` gives the BGP paths of the route table section that are not backup paths.

The ports of the summary of a router are its physical ports and bundles with a MAC in `show interfaces` and a lowered interface; a bundle member is its bundle, and the layer 3 addresses of a port are by the dot1q VLAN of its sub-interfaces. A frame enters a port with a link at `handoff/<port>`, which sends the MACs of the port and of its active HSRP groups to the ingress of the port or of the sub-interface of the tag; an egress over a link sets the MAC of the interface and the dot1q VLAN, and an egress with no link keeps its tag and has no link. An HSRP group is active, not active or unknown from the state word of its IPv4 row in `show hsrp`; the virtual address of an active group is a local route and its virtual MAC enters at the hand-off, and a group with no readable state, a line that is not read or a group address that differs gives `Unknown` for the packets to its virtual address and its virtual MAC. The virtual MAC is the MAC of the `mac-address` line of the group; an active group or a group of unknown state with no such line makes each frame of its port unknown. The tests use the synthetic running config and text sections in `crates/rsim-ingest/tests/fixtures/iosxr/`; the config has fake secrets.

## Commands

| Task | Command |
|---|---|
| IOS-XR ingestion tests | `cargo test -p rsim-ingest --test iosxr` |
| IOS-XR layer 3 tests | `cargo test -p rsim-ingest --test iosxr_l3` |
| IOS-XR link and HSRP tests | `cargo test -p rsim-ingest --test iosxr_links` |
| Coverage of an IOS-XR running config | `cargo run -p rsim-ingest --example iosxr_coverage < running-config.txt`. Each printed line goes through the mask of the secrets. |
