# `rsim-routing`

`rsim-routing` holds the routing calculation. It depends on `rsim-core` and `rsim-functions`, and it has no I/O, no clock and no randomness. `install.rs` selects the route of each prefix from its candidates by the administrative distance and resolves the gateways of the candidates over the installed routes. `platform.rs` holds `Platform` and the administrative distance of each `Origin`.

`ospf/` holds the OSPF calculation. `ospf/config.rs` holds the OSPF config of a router in one form for all vendors, and `ospf/build.rs` builds the databases of the twin from these configs. `tests/ospf_oracle.rs` and `tests/ospf_build_oracle.rs` check the calculation and the builder against a second shortest-path calculation in the test. A `Database` is the link-state database of one process of a device in one VRF and one `Version`: the router ID, the stated area types, the interfaces, the neighbours and the LSAs by `LsaKey` (the area with the AS-external LSAs last, the type, the link-state ID and the advertising router), each `Lsa` with the span of its lines. `Database::insert` keeps the newer instance of an LSA (RFC 2328 13.1). The OSPFv3 types share the variants of the OSPFv2 types of the same role: `Summary` is the inter-area-prefix LSA, `AsbrSummary` the inter-area-router LSA, `External` the AS-external LSA and `NssaExternal` the NSSA LSA; `Link` and `IntraAreaPrefix` are OSPFv3 only.

`compute(database, router ID, profile)` in `ospf/spf.rs` follows RFC 2328 section 16 with RFC 3101 for NSSA areas and RFC 5340 for OSPFv3, with one shortest-path code: a network vertex is the link-state ID of an OSPFv2 network LSA, or the designated router and its interface ID for OSPFv3; the prefixes of OSPFv3 come from the intra-area-prefix LSAs, and its next hops from the link-local address of the link LSA of the neighbour. A next hop has the gateway and the interface of the calculating router, from its interface address (OSPFv2) or its interface ID (OSPFv3) in `Database::interfaces`. The subnets of the calculating router are not routes. An area border router reads only the summary LSAs of the backbone. `OspfProfile::of(Platform)` holds the RFC 1583 setting, the reference bandwidth and the cost rounding of HST `simul/platforms.py`; IOS-XE is RFC 1583 compatible.

An LSA that the calculation needs and the database lacks is a `Gap`, listed once by type, link-state ID and area. A gap of the shortest path tree reaches the intra-area routes with a cost of at least the cost at which the tree meets it, and each inter-area and external route (`GapReach::FromCost`); the missing router LSA of the calculating router reaches each route (`GapReach::All`); a border router or an AS boundary router with no LSA reaches the prefixes of its summary or external LSAs (`GapReach::Prefixes`). `Database::gaps` holds the LSAs that the builder `ospf/build.rs` leaves out in the flooding scope of the database, and `compute` adds them to the gaps of the calculation: the left-out LSAs of known prefixes reach those prefixes (`GapReach::Prefixes`), left-out summary LSAs of prefixes that are not known reach each inter-area and external route (`GapReach::InterArea`), and left-out external LSAs of prefixes that are not known reach each external route (`GapReach::External`); the last 2 also reach each prefix with no route. The NSSA border router with the highest router ID that the NSSA calculation of each other router of the NSSA reaches translates the NSSA LSAs with the P bit, except the default route, into AS-external LSAs (RFC 3101 3.1 and 3.2). A left-out process has a gap of its interface prefixes and its redistributed prefixes in each of its areas, and an area border router carries this gap into its other areas. A route that a gap reaches has the reason in `Route::unknown`, and `Calculation::absent_gap` gives the gap that makes a prefix with no route unknown. A virtual link of the calculating router is not computed and gives a note. `ospf/change.rs` holds the changes of an OSPFv2 database: an interface cost, an adjacency down and an interface in another area; each gives a new database.

The tests in `tests/ospf.rs` are the cases of HST `tests/test_simul_ospf.py` with documentation addresses and the OSPFv3 cases; the expected outputs are not confirmed against a device.

`bgp/` holds the BGP types, the policy evaluator, the profiles, the decision function, the propagation and the convergence detection. Its input is one `BgpProcess` for each device and VRF: the ASN, the router ID, the cluster ID, the confederation, the options (`BgpOptions`, where `None` takes the default of the profile), the neighbours, the `network` and `aggregate` statements and the policies and lists by name (`PolicySet`). A `BgpPath` holds the attributes of one path, the peer with its `SessionKind`, the IGP cost of the next hop and the flag `attributes_collected`. A `RouteKey` is the family (IPv4 unicast, IPv6 unicast or the EVPN IP prefix route), the route distinguisher and the prefix. `Asn` reads the asplain and the asdot form. `AsPath::len` counts an AS_SET as 1 and a confederation segment as 0. `stage.rs` holds the trait `RoutingStage` with `reads()`, the objects that a stage reads. `PolicyStage` and `DecisionStage` implement it.

`bgp/policy.rs` evaluates a route-map with `continue` or an IOS-XR RPL policy with `if`, `pass`, `drop`, `done` and `apply`. `bgp/lists.rs` holds the prefix lists for IPv4 and IPv6, the community lists and the AS-path lists. `bgp/pattern.rs` matches the Cisco regular expression subset of digits, the space, `:`, `.`, `^`, `$`, `_`, `*`, `+`, `?`, `[]`, `()` and `|` with a state-set automaton. An unhandled term, an unhandled list line, a pattern outside the subset or a list that the set does not hold has an unknown match. The evaluation runs with the match true and with the match false, and the result is `Unknown` with the reason "policy clause not evaluated" only when the 2 results differ. An unhandled action that a run reaches gives `Unknown`. `neighbor_policy` applies the policy of a neighbour, the eBGP rule with no policy and the result of an undefined policy name.

`BgpProfile::of(Platform)` in `bgp/profile.rs` holds the decision order, the deterministic MED and missing-as-worst defaults, the eBGP rule with no policy, the rank of a local aggregate, the maximum paths (a build value on FRR), the IPv4 unicast activation, the result of an undefined neighbour policy and the route that a later match reads. The module comment gives the source of each value. `decide` in `bgp/decision.rs` follows RFC 4271 9.1.2.2 with the vendor steps. The decision does not model the oldest-path step. 2 eBGP paths that are equal through the IGP cost give `Decision::Tie`, unless compare-routerid is set. With deterministic MED the candidates are grouped by the neighbour AS. With no deterministic MED and no always-compare-med, the candidates are compared in input order, and `order_winners` gives each candidate that is the best path in some input order, from a calculation over the subsets of the top candidates, when a pair of the top candidates is decided by the MED and the top candidates have more than 1 neighbour AS. The top candidates are the candidates that no candidate beats in a step before the MED. More than `ORDER_BOUND` (12) top candidates give `Unknown` with the reason "candidate order not computed". A path whose first segment after the confederation segments is an AS_SET compares its MED with no other path, as in FRR `aspath_cmp_left`. More than 1 candidate with a path whose attributes are not collected gives `Unknown` with the reason "path attributes not collected", and no profile gives "no bgp profile".

`bgp/session.rs` holds the `Session` between 2 processes of the twin and the transfer of a best path over it. `export` applies the rule of the source peer, the well-known communities, the iBGP rule with route reflection (RFC 4456: ORIGINATOR_ID and CLUSTER_LIST), the attribute rules of each session kind, the export policy, the AS path prepend with `local-as` and the confederation (RFC 5065), the next hop and the community flags. `receive` applies the AS path, ORIGINATOR_ID and CLUSTER_LIST loop checks, the peer of the session and the import policy. `SessionStage` implements `RoutingStage` for one direction of a session.

`bgp/propagate.rs` holds the input `BgpInput` (the processes, the sessions and the `Seed` paths from peers outside the twin), the trait `IgpView` (the resolution and the IGP cost of a next hop, and the route of a `network` statement; a next hop that resolves only through a BGP route has an unknown resolution), the originations (`network_offer`, `aggregate_offer` with `summary-only` and `as-set`, `OriginateStage`) and the variables of a prefix group: one route key at one process. The candidates of a variable are its `network` path, its aggregate path, its seeds and the accepted paths of the sessions, in this order. When more than 1 candidate is the best path in some order of receipt, each of them is a tied path of the selection. The AS path of an `as-set` aggregate (`aggregate_as_path`, RFC 4271 9.2.2.2 and appendix F.6) is an AS_CONFED_SET of the confederation ASs of the contributors, the longest leading AS_SEQUENCE of the contributors, and an AS_SET of the other ASs. A next hop that does not resolve gives no candidate, and an unknown resolution gives an unknown candidate. An unknown candidate makes the selection unknown. An unknown best path is not sent to a neighbour with `shutdown`, and it is not sent to an iBGP neighbour that is not a route-reflector client when each path of the variable comes from such a neighbour.

`compute(input, igp)` in `bgp/converge.rs` runs each prefix group alone: an aggregate with the keys inside it, and each key whose prefix holds a possible next hop with all keys. The forward pass runs synchronous rounds from no best path, and Brent's algorithm finds the fixed point or the cycle by exact comparison of the states. The uniqueness check runs the greedy construction of Griffin, Shepherd and Wilfong over the possible candidates of each variable. It applies only when no process with a candidate in the group has a route-reflector client, no candidate is unknown, and no pair of candidates is decided by the MED with no always-compare-med, by the IGP cost, or by a tie. Else the search assigns each variable that is not fixed a source and keeps the assignments in which each source gives the selection of its variable, or a tied path of it. An unknown selection needs a grounded reason in the assignment: the variables with an unknown source run synchronous rounds from no path, and each of them must reach an unknown selection before the rounds repeat a state. The search has no bound. 2 stable assignments are distinct when they differ in the installed paths or in a sent path of a variable. The result of each group is `Converged`, `MultipleStableOutcomes` with the outcomes, `NoConvergence` with the cycle of the forward pass, `Unknown` with the reason "convergence not determined" for 1 outcome of the search with an unknown route, or `Unknown` with the reason "internal error" for 0 outcomes of the search at a fixed point of the forward pass. A group whose greedy construction fixes each variable is `Converged` with no note. 1 outcome of the search with no unknown route gives `Converged` with the note "not safe under all timings" where the greedy construction stops at a dispute wheel, with the wheel, or where the forward pass cycles, and else with the note "safety under all timings not checked". An `Unknown` result with 1 outcome keeps the routes of the outcome. The routes of the other results that are not `Converged` are unknown: for `MultipleStableOutcomes` each variable with a path in an outcome, and for the others each variable with a possible candidate in the closure of the uniqueness check. `BgpResult` holds the routes, the groups, the round events and the search events, the worst result and the affected keys. `install_candidates` gives the `Candidate` values of the unicast routes for `install`: a BGP route through the multipath next hops with the distance of the profile, a discard route for an aggregate, no candidate for a `network` path, and an unknown route with the lowest eBGP distance.

`tests/bgp_convergence.rs` checks GOOD GADGET, BAD GADGET, DISAGREE, an instance with 1 solution and a dispute wheel, the type I and type II oscillations of RFC 3345 figures 1 and 3 with and without always-compare-med, DISAGREE inside one reflector cluster and between 2 clusters, a reflector with no path of the group, a chain of 201 routers, the MED cycle of 1 router with and without deterministic MED, a route of a group with no convergence that no round of the cycle holds, BAD GADGET with an unhandled import term, a MED decision over more candidates than the bound, an unhandled import term on the first path of a sender only, and 162 stable-paths instances against an enumeration of the stable assignments in the test. `tests/bgp_propagation.rs` checks the export and receipt rules with `local-as` and the confederation, the originations, the seeds, the prefix groups, the multipath, the EVPN IP prefix route and the install form. `tests/bgp_stage_reads.rs` changes each object of each process and checks that each stage gives the same output after the change of an object that it does not declare.

`tests/bgp_prefix_oracle.rs`, `tests/bgp_pattern_oracle.rs` and `tests/bgp_decision_oracle.rs` check the prefix lists, the patterns and the decision against a second implementation in the test. The second implementations are a bit-string matcher over small IPv4 and IPv6 spaces, a backtracking matcher over each AS path up to length 4, and a pairwise ranking over each order of up to 5 generated candidates. The order winners are checked against the best paths of each order of up to 6 generated candidates. `tests/bgp_policy.rs` and `tests/bgp_decision.rs` hold the cases of HST `tests/test_simul_matchlist.py`, `test_simul_prefixlist.py`, `test_simul_policy.py`, `test_simul_bestpath.py` and `test_bgp_asdot.py` with documentation addresses, and the values of each profile. The expected outputs are not confirmed against a device.
