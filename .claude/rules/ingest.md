# `rsim-ingest`

`rsim-ingest` reads the snapshot store and gives function instances. It has no network code, no async runtime, no clock and no randomness. `stanza.rs` reads JSON, and indented config text such as a Cisco running config, into a tree of stanzas with the line ranges of the canonical form. In a classification table, a segment with a space or a final `..` is a word pattern for the line of an indented config, for example `interface */ip address ..`.

`mask.rs` gives the masked lines of a section for the source lines of the API, by vendor and format: a JSON section of VyOS (`vyos/mask.rs`), UniFi (`unifi/mask.rs`), VeloCloud (`velocloud/mask.rs`) or NX-OS is masked by its keys, where the value of a secret key is masked whole with each key and value below it and the lines stay those of the canonical form; the common rule follows the `SECRET_KEYS` and `SECRET_WORDS` of the HST redaction, with an SNMP community below `snmp`, a `{name, value}` setting record and a crypt hash under any key, VyOS adds its words and each key that ends with `key` or `keys` and is not a public key, UniFi adds each `x_` key, where `x_fingerprint` and the `x_has_*` keys are secrets only by the other rules, each key of the file of a VPN client (a key with `configuration_file` or a key that ends with `_configuration`), and, below a key with `dhcp` and `option` such as `wan_dhcp_options` (`JsonMask::value_records`), the `value` of each record and each string, VeloCloud adds each key with `activationkey` or `md5` and each key that ends with `key` or `keys` and is not a public key, and NX-OS adds each key with `auth` or `keystring` that does not end with `type` or `mode`, such as the authentication text of an HSRP or VRRP group; below a subtree of `vyos::mask::SUBTREES`, a part of the config in which the user names the secret (the environment, arguments, command, entry point and labels of a container, the environment and arguments of an event handler script, the arguments of a scheduled task, the `openvpn-option` lines and the string `protocols nhrp tunnel * authentication` of VyOS 1.5), each value is masked and the keys stay; `mask::url_secrets` masks in each JSON key and value and in each word of a text line each part of a word between commas alone: the whole userinfo of a URL up to the last `@` of the part (`scheme://<secret>@host`), except a user name with no `:` for `ssh` and `git`, the text before the last `@` before the first `://` of the part when it holds a `:` (`<secret>@host`), and the value of a query parameter whose name is a secret key, ends with `sig` or `signature`, or is one of `token`, `password`, `pass`, `pwd`, `key`, `secret`, `auth`, `apikey`, `access_token`, `sig` and `signature`; a text section of NX-OS, IOS-XR or IOS-XE is masked line by line by the mask of its vendor, whose secret words include `server-key`, `area-password`, `domain-password`, `text-authentication`, `community-map`, `key-octet-string`, `hello-authentication`, `user-pin`, `pin`, `pmk` (the key of `sap pmk` under `cts manual`) and, on NX-OS, `encrypted`, the wireless keys `set-key` and `wpa-psk` and the container options `run-opts`, `prepend-pkg-opts`, `docker-run-opts` and `docker-run-cmd`; `mask::cisco_secret_start` reads each word in lower case with the quotes and the punctuation at its ends trimmed, and masks from a word `name=value` whose name is a secret word or a secret key of the common rule, with the words after the host of `snmp-server host` and of a `host` line (the `snmp-server vrf` submode) and the value of `event manager environment` masked (`mask::cisco_kept_words`), and a line that the mask does not change keeps its spaces; each other vendor and format has no mask.

`coverage.rs` classifies each stanza as handled, known-irrelevant or unhandled from a table of paths, and gives the coverage report. `collected.rs` reads the rows of the collected tables.

`facts.rs` gives the facts of each device of a snapshot (`DeviceFacts`, in `Estate::facts`): the vendor and the time of the manifest, the management address of the manifest, and the platform, the OS version and the serial with the span of each, from the VyOS `version` lines `Version:`, `Hardware model:` and `Hardware S/N:`, the NX-OS `version` keys `chassis_id`, `nxos_ver_str` else `sys_ver_str` and `proc_board_id`, the IOS-XR `version` template and the PID and SN of the row `Rack 0` of `inventory`, the IOS-XE `version` template (`VERSION`, the first `HARDWARE` and the first `SERIAL`), and for each device record of a UniFi `devices` section, by the name that the lowering gives the device, its `model`, `version`, `serial` and `ip`; a UniFi console has its own entry only when the section gives no device; and for each edge row of a VeloCloud `edges` section, else of its `edges_v2` section, by the twin device of the edge, its `modelNumber`, `softwareVersion` and `serialNumber` with no management address. A fact with no section is `not_collected`, and a fact that no reader gives, or a placeholder (`facts::placeholder_serial` for a serial: a word of the HST list, or one character repeated; `facts::placeholder_text` for a platform or an OS version: a firmware placeholder such as `Default string`, `System Product Name` or `0`, or one character 2 or more times), is `not_read`.

`bgp.rs` reads the BGP session sections of each vendor into `SessionRow`s with a `SectionOutcome`: `Complete`, `CompleteEmpty`, `Partial`, `Failed` or `NotCaptured`, with the pairs of a VRF and an address family that the read covers as its scope (`Scope::covers(vrf, family)`). A section that is not collected or that the device does not have is `NotCaptured`, a failed or old read is `Failed`, and an answer with row-shaped lines that are not read is `Partial`, or `Failed` if no row is read. The scope of VyOS is each pair of a VRF and an address family that the answer lists with its peers, of NX-OS IPv4 unicast in the listed VRFs, and of IOS-XR and IOS-XE IPv4 unicast in each VRF whose summary is read; the IOS-XE rows go through the TextFSM template after the removal of the mark `*` of a dynamic peer. `estate::build` reads these sections with the status of each (`estate::session_inputs`), and `estate::assemble_with` takes them.

`ospf/` reads the OSPF sections of NX-OS, IOS-XE, IOS-XR and VyOS into the `Database`s of `rsim-routing`, one for each VRF, process and version (`ospf::read`, `DeviceOspf`), with a `SectionOutcome` of each version whose scope is the VRFs of the databases with the family `ipv4` or `ipv6`. `ospf::inputs` reads the sections of each device of a snapshot and `ospf::of_snapshot` gives the databases of each device. The process section gives the router ID and the area types, the interface section the interfaces, and each database section the LSAs of one type with the span of each LSA. The outcome is `NotCaptured` when no database section is collected or each is absent, `Failed` when a read failed or when an answer has parts that are not read and no LSA, `Partial` when some sections are absent or an LSA is not read, else `Complete` or `CompleteEmpty`. `ospf/json.rs` reads NX-OS with the keys of HST and the FRR answers of VyOS, and `ospf/text.rs` reads the Cisco text after HST `ospf_lsa_detail_rows`, with the OSPFv3 lines; the VRF of an IOS process is the VRF of its `Connected to MPLS VPN Superbackbone, VRF` line, else of its `router ospf <process> vrf <name>` line of the running config. The fields are not confirmed on a device. The tests in `tests/ospf.rs` read the same database in the format of each vendor from `tests/fixtures/ospf/`.

`bgp_peers.rs` holds the BGP config of a device after the inheritance (`BgpConfig`: the process of each VRF with its AS and confederation, and the neighbours and listen ranges with their `Settings`), the AS reader of asplain and asdot (`parse_asn`), and `facts`, which gives one `BgpPeerFact` for each configured neighbour, each listen range and each observed peer with no neighbour, joined with the session rows by VRF and address; an observed peer in exactly one listen range of its VRF whose inheritance resolves takes the settings of the range. A source of the inheritance that is not defined or that comes back to itself is a gap (`inheritance gap: ...`), and the settings that the neighbour does not state are then not known: the AS of the peer, the presented local AS and the shutdown state. `remote-as external` and `auto`, NX-OS `remote-as route-map` and a `local-as` or confederation line that is not read give an `Unknown` AS with a reason that starts with `asn not resolved`; the AS of the session row is the separate field `observed_asn`. The presented AS is the `local-as` of the neighbour, with the outward AS too under `dual-as`, else in a confederation the AS of the process toward a member and the identifier toward another peer, else the AS of the process.

Each vendor module has a `bgp.rs` that reads its neighbour lines and adds their handled entries to its table before the routing entries, so the process line, the VRF and address-family stanzas, the neighbours, the groups, the templates and their remote-as, update-source, shutdown, local-as, inheritance, activation, listen range and confederation lines are handled in each VRF, and the other lines below them keep the state of the routing lines of their VRF, except the lines that the reader of each vendor in `bgp_config.rs` lowers: VyOS `protocols bgp` with its peer groups and listen ranges, where the process of VyOS 1.3 is not read; NX-OS templates `peer`, `peer-session` and `peer-policy` with `inherit`; IOS-XR `neighbor-group` and `session-group` with `use`, where a neighbour takes its session-group, else the one of its neighbor-group, after the neighbor-group; IOS-XE `neighbor <peer> <keyword>` lines, peer groups, `template peer-session` and `template peer-policy`, and `bgp listen range`, where a neighbour of the global table is active in IPv4 unicast by `BGP_DEFAULT_IPV4_UNICAST` of `iosxe/profile.rs` unless `no bgp default ipv4-unicast`. `RouteTables::push_handled_vrfs` adds the handled entry of a VRF stanza of a routing process.

`bgp_config.rs` holds the BGP config of a device for the BGP calculation (`DeviceBgp`): one `rsim_routing::bgp::BgpProcess` for each VRF with a known AS, and the gaps (`Gap`), each with its VRF, the unicast families that it reaches and its text. `bgp_config::neighbor` lowers a neighbour of `bgp_peers.rs` with the settings of its unicast families and of `l2vpn evpn` (`bgp_config::FAMILIES`, `AfSettings`: the import and export policy, `route-reflector-client`, `next-hop-self`, the communities that it sends and `remove-private-as`). A listen range, a neighbour named by its interface, an inheritance gap, a remote AS other than a number or `internal`, an unread `local-as` and families with different settings are gaps. A process with no router-id line has a router ID that is not known. A line of a process, a neighbour or a group that the reader of the vendor does not lower is a gap, except a line of `bgp_config::no_effect` (the description, the logging, the timers, the passwords, BFD, graceful restart, the soft reconfiguration, the prefix limit, the multihop and transport settings, the capabilities and the event history) and a line below an address family other than IPv4 and IPv6 unicast. The readers lower the `redistribute` statements of connected, static and OSPF routes, `default-information originate`, and the route distinguisher and the route targets of a VRF of the rules of each vendor. A line that imports or exports routes between VRFs or between EVPN and a VRF in another form is a gap of its VRF. The readers of VyOS (`vyos/bgp_config.rs`), NX-OS (`nxos/bgp_config.rs`), IOS-XR (`iosxr/bgp_config.rs`) and IOS-XE (`iosxe/bgp_config.rs`) mark each line that they lower as read, and their table entries make those lines handled.

`bgp_build.rs` holds the BGP calculation of the twin (`bgp_build::build`, `BgpBuild` in `Estate::bgp_routing`), on the VyOS routers, the NX-OS switches and the IOS-XR and IOS-XE devices. A configured neighbour with a peer address is one end of a session when its far end of `links::bgp_pairs` is `Paired` with a configured neighbour of a lowered device and both neighbours are lowered. A neighbour whose far end is `Outside` gives seeds (`rsim_routing::bgp::Seed`) from one source: the rows of its received table of the section `bgp_routes`, with `received` true, so the import policy runs on them, else the rows of its accepted table, with `received` false, when the table is `Complete` or `CompleteEmpty`. Else each collected BGP route of its VRF whose next hop is the peer address is a seed with `received` false and attributes that are not collected, with the AS of the peer as its AS path on an eBGP session. A seed from a table takes the next hop, the AS path, the origin, the MED, the local preference and the communities of its row, and of an accepted row also the weight. Its attributes are collected only when the row holds an AS path, an origin and communities, so a seed from a text table of IOS-XR or IOS-XE has attributes that are not collected. The router ID of an eBGP seed is the router ID of the neighbour in the neighbour detail (`bgp_neighbors::router_ids`), else not known. An iBGP seed has no router ID. Each other far end is a gap of the VRF of the neighbour. A gap covers each process in the component of its VRF over the sessions, the far ends of the neighbours and the leaks between the VRFs of a device (the EVPN export and import of a VRF with the default VRF, and the route targets between 2 VRFs), for the families of the gap. Its reason is `bgp gap: <text> on <device>`, with `in vrf <vrf>` for a VRF other than the default VRF, of the first gap of the component by device, VRF and text. The IGP view (`bgp_build::Igp`) of a VRF (`IgpTable`) is the install stage of the connected, local, static, OSPFv2 and OSPFv3 candidates of the guard of the vendor, with the collected routes that the guard does not compare and that are not BGP routes (`guard::igp`), and an unknown candidate of each BGP prefix of the VRF at the lower of the eBGP and the iBGP distance of the platform. The BGP prefixes of a VRF are the prefixes of its collected BGP routes, the `network`, aggregate and seed keys of its component, `0.0.0.0/0` and `::/0`. A seeded collected route has the default distance of its kind, and a collected route that is not computed is an unknown route with the lowest distance of the platform other than a connected or a local route (`guard::lowest_distance`). The first lowering of each NX-OS, IOS-XR and IOS-XE device and `vyos::lower::igp_tables` give the candidates, so only a VRF with collected route rows has a view, and `bgp_build::build` installs them with the BGP prefixes. A next hop resolves by the longest route of the table other than a default route. A connected or a local route gives the cost 0, a static route the cost 0 and an OSPF route its metric of `OspfRoutes::metrics`. An unknown route, a discard route, a route of another protocol and no route give an unknown resolution. When an OSPF version has a process of the VRF in the config and is not computed in full (`OspfRoutes::incomplete`: no database, a database not read in full, or a gap), each lookup of that family that does not resolve through a connected or local route is unknown, and `has_route` gives `None` for each prefix other than a connected or local route. `has_route` reads the install of the candidates with no BGP prefix. The `redistribute` statements of a process read the installed routes of the view (`IgpTable::plain`) as `rsim_routing::bgp::IgpRoute`: the connected, the static and the OSPF routes with the process and the route type of `OspfRoutes::route_types`. A route of a prefix whose BGP route the round before installs is not offered, and a route of a prefix whose installed BGP route was unknown is unknown. A redistribution of an OSPF version that is not computed in full is a gap of its family. The VRFs of an NX-OS switch take the tunnel end of the first lowering (`Lowered::evpn_next_hop`, the `routed_source` of the NVE plan) as the next hop of their EVPN routes. `rsim_routing::bgp::compute` runs on the processes, the sessions, the seeds and the installed routes, `install_candidates` gives the BGP candidates of each VRF (`VrfBgp`), and `overlay_candidates` gives the EVPN routes that a VRF imports (`VrfBgp::overlay`), where each candidate under a gap is unknown with the reason of the gap. The NX-OS guard lowers an overlay candidate to the NVE interface with each tunnel end as the gateway, and to an unknown route when the tunnel end of the switch or the layer 3 VNI of the VRF is not lowered. `BgpBuild::installed` holds the BGP routes that the route table of each VRF installs over the IGP view, with the iBGP routes marked, and the prefixes of the unknown ones.

The estate runs the rounds of the routing calculation with `rsim_routing::rounds::run`. The first round builds the OSPF databases with no installed BGP route in each VRF with a BGP process, and runs BGP over the IGP views of the first lowerings. Each next round builds the OSPF databases with the installed BGP routes of the round before (`RouterInput::bgp`), lowers the IGP view of each device with BGP again, and runs BGP with the installed routes of the round before. The rounds end when a round installs the BGP routes of the round before. A cycle is a gap `routing rounds: a cycle of <n> rounds from round <r>` of each process with a `redistribute` statement, and the OSPF build then does not evaluate a redistribution of BGP. The note `routing rounds` of `estate` gives the count of rounds or the cycle. The database guard compares the databases of the last round with the collected databases. A collected BGP route of a key of a group whose convergence is `Unknown` is not computed with the reason of the group. The round events, the search events, the groups that do not converge with no note and the gaps are notes of their devices and of `estate`.

`DeviceBgp` of each vendor comes from the second reading of the config in `estate::assemble_pass`, with the file of the config in the spans. `Config::bgp_routing` holds the first reading with no file.

`bgp_routes.rs` reads the section `bgp_routes` (`bgp_routes::read`) into one `Table` for each VRF, neighbour and direction (`Direction`: the received, the accepted and the advertised table), with its `PathRow`s and an outcome of `bgp.rs`. NX-OS and VyOS store a JSON object of VRF, address and direction with `answer` or `failed`: the NX-OS answer is the `TABLE_vrf` tree of HST, with `{}` as an empty table, and the VyOS answer is the FRR form, which is `Failed` with no table key, a key that is not an IPv4 prefix, or a prefix counter (`totalPrefixCounter`, `numRoutes`) that does not agree with the table. IOS-XE and IOS-XR store the text answers after a line `NEIGHBOR: <vrf> <address> <direction>`, which `bgp_table.rs` reads by the columns of their header, after HST `cisco_bgp_table.py`: a network alone on its line wraps to the next line, a path with a blank network takes the network above it, a network with no length gets its classful length, and the IOS-XR advertised table has the columns `Network`, `Next Hop`, `From` and `AS Path`. A blank text answer or one with no row-shaped line is `CompleteEmpty`. A `%` line, a prefix counter (`Total number of prefixes`, `Processed <n> prefixes`) that does not agree with the prefixes of the rows, and row-shaped lines of which none is read make the table `Failed`. The text tables hold no communities. A table that is not read whole is a note of its device. `estate::session_inputs` reads the section with the session sections.

`bgp_neighbors.rs` reads the router ID of each neighbour of IOS-XE and IOS-XR from the neighbour detail, the section `bgp_neighbors` and on IOS-XR also `bgp_vrf_neighbors`, after HST `cisco_bgp.parse_neighbor_router_ids`. A block starts with `BGP neighbor is <address>` with an optional `vrf <name>`, and its `remote router ID` line gives the router ID. The router ID 0.0.0.0 gives none, and the first block of a neighbour gives its router ID. The tests in `tests/bgp_routes.rs` read the fixtures `tests/fixtures/bgp/routes-*` of each vendor.

`route_tables.rs` holds the choice of the route table of each VRF (`RouteTables`): a VRF with collected route rows forwards on them, and the routing lines of its config are known-irrelevant; a VRF with no rows forwards on the connected and static routes of its config, and its routing lines are unhandled; with a dynamic routing protocol in its config, only its connected and local routes give an answer, because a learned route can be more specific than a static route, and each other destination is unknown with the code `ReasonCode::NotCollected` and the reason `vrf route table not collected`. The table builder of each vendor takes the VRFs with rows and the VRFs with a dynamic protocol, and the profile of each vendor maps the protocol word of a collected route to its `RouteProtocol`.

Each vendor has an OSPF config reader (`ospf_config.rs`) that gives the `RouterConfig` of `rsim-routing`. `estate::build` reads the OSPF config of each VyOS, NX-OS, IOS-XR and IOS-XE device with the connected and static prefixes and the addresses of each VRF (`ospf::inputs`), builds the databases of the twin with `rsim_routing::ospf::build`, compares each built database with the collected database of the same VRF, version and process (`Estate::ospf_guard`), and puts the built databases in place of the collected ones for the computed OSPF routes (`DeviceOspf::built`). The notes of the builder go to the notes of each device.

The RIB guard of each VRF with collected route rows compares the computed route table with the collected route table (`rsim_guard::compare`) and marks the routes of the collected table (`rsim_guard::mark`). `Estate::guard` holds the rows of each device. The computed route table is the install stage of `rsim-routing` (`install`) on the connected routes, the local routes, the static routes of the config, the OSPF routes and, the BGP routes of `VrfBgp` on VyOS, NX-OS, IOS-XR and IOS-XE, each with its administrative distance. Each next hop of a static route is one candidate: VyOS takes the `distance` of each next hop, NX-OS and IOS-XE the preference of each `ip route` line, and IOS-XR the distance of each route line of `router static` (`Config::static_routes`), else 1. A static route with options that are not lowered is one unknown candidate with the lowest distance of its next hops. The install stage resolves a gateway with no interface by the longest prefix match over the routes that it installs, with the flag `COMPUTED_THROUGH_DEFAULT` of the vendor profile for a default route. A candidate whose gateway does not resolve is not installed, so a floating static route installs. The OSPF candidates come from `ospf::routes::OspfRoutes`, the calculation of each database of the VRF: a route that depends on a gap of the database, whose next hop interface is not known, or whose prefix more than one process gives, is unknown. The OSPFv3 routes are candidates only on VyOS, whose collected table holds IPv6. NX-OS, IOS-XR and IOS-XE collect IPv4 tables. The class of a collected route comes from its protocol word: the connected, local and static routes are compared, a VyOS `kernel` route is not computed with the reason `kernel route`, and each other protocol is not computed with the reason `<protocol> not computed`. On VyOS, NX-OS, IOS-XR and IOS-XE, a collected BGP route of a VRF with BGP routes from the calculation is seeded when the computed best path and each multipath path of its device and VRF come from a peer outside the twin and their next hops are the collected next hops, not computed with the reason of the gap when a gap covers it, not computed with `no bgp process of the vrf` with no process, not computed with the reason of the unknown route of the calculation (for example `multiple stable outcomes`), and else compared. The guard compares the selected routes by their kind and next hops. Neither side holds a metric. A collected OSPF route (VyOS `ospf` and `ospf6`) is compared, or not computed with the reason when its version is not read in full, when the VRF has no database (`no OSPF database of the VRF`), when the computed route depends on a gap, or when a gap covers a prefix with no computed route. On VyOS, a collected route of an interface whose address comes from PPPoE, DHCP, DHCPv6, IPv6 autoconfiguration or DHCPv6 prefix delegation is seeded when the computed table has no route of its prefix. On IOS-XR and IOS-XE, a collected static route of a VRF with no static route of the config is seeded. The VRFs with no collected rows forward on the same install stage with no OSPF candidates. The lowering notes of the static routes are not kept when the guard runs on a VRF that forwards on its collected table.

`scenario.rs` holds the what-if layers and the forwarding table of a scenario. A `Layer` is `LinkDown` with a device and an interface. Its text is `link-down <device>:<interface>` (`Layer::text`, `Layer::parse`), and its span is the file `layer <n>: <text>` at line 1, with `n` from 1. `estate::assemble_scenario` and `estate::build_scenario` first build the base, a snapshot twin that also keeps the computed table, the collected table and the guard rows of each VRF with a guard (`Pass::Base`, `Estate::base`). Then they build the scenario (`Pass::Scenario`). A link-down layer takes down the named interface and the far end of each link of the interface in the link join of the base (`Estate::links`). A sub-interface with no link of its own, for example `eth1.10`, takes down the sub-interface with the same number of the far end of each link of its interface, for example `eth3.10`. The config of the far device need not have it (`DeviceScenario::far`). Another interface with no link goes down alone. The build refuses a layer on a device that is not a VyOS, NX-OS, IOS-XR or IOS-XE device, and a layer whose far end is such a device, with the vendor of `Estate::vendors`. It also refuses an interface that the config does not have. The scenario sets the interface and its sub-interfaces down in the typed config before the lowering: `disabled` on VyOS, with each PPPoE interface over it, `admin_up` on NX-OS and IOS-XE, and `shutdown` on IOS-XR. It sets them down in the OSPF config too (`scenario::down_ospf`), and a down port ends no link.

In a scenario, the guard of each VRF gives the forwarding table of `scenario::forward`. A prefix with a BGP route in the collected table, in the computed table of the base or in the computed table of the scenario is unknown with the reason `routing not recomputed`. The scenario runs no BGP calculation: its computed table of VyOS, NX-OS, IOS-XR and IOS-XE has an unknown BGP candidate with that reason for each BGP route of the computed table of the base (`VrfBgp::scenario`). A collected route that is not computed is unknown with the reason `routing not recomputed`. A seeded route stays. A prefix whose computed route is the same and known in the base and in the scenario keeps its collected route. A prefix with no route in the base takes the computed route of the scenario. A changed prefix takes the computed route of the scenario, or is removed, when the guard row of the base is a match. Else it is unknown with the reason `route table guard mismatch`. A prefix with no guard row matches when the collected and the computed route of the base are the same and known. A kept route with a next hop on a down interface is unknown with the reason `route table guard mismatch`. A collected table with no guard, for example the underlay table of an NX-OS switch, takes `scenario::fallback`, and so does each route lookup of a UniFi, VeloCloud or Equinix device: each learned route is unknown with the reason `routing not recomputed`. Each route that the scenario changes has the spans of the layers. The scenario keeps the guard rows of the base. With no layer, the twin is the snapshot twin. The tests in `tests/scenario.rs` run on the synthetic NX-OS OSPF triangle of `tests/support/triangle.rs`, which the CLI tests also write to a store, and `tests/nxos_vxlan_scenario.rs` runs a link-down layer on one of the 2 uplinks of the VXLAN underlay of 2 NX-OS leaves.

The tests in `tests/bgp_routing.rs` run on the synthetic NX-OS switch and IOS-XR routers of `tests/fixtures/bgp/`: an eBGP pair, 2 iBGP sessions over the loopbacks and OSPF with a route reflector, an import policy that sets the local preference, a seed from a peer outside the twin from the collected route and from its received table through the import policy, a redistribution that is a gap of its component, a BGP route that matches in the base and is unknown in a scenario, a reflected route of a client with no router ID that makes its group not computed, a route of an outside route reflector with a next hop inside the twin that is seeded, a collected route through an outside peer with a best path from inside whose guard row is `differ`, an IPv6 next hop learned by OSPFv3, and the local preference of the received table of an iBGP peer outside the twin. The floating static route of the config of x3 leaves the OSPF cost of its next hop. The unit tests of `bgp_build.rs` check the IGP view with a floating static route, a collected OSPF host route that is not computed inside a connected route, a collected route of a protocol that the twin does not model, a BGP prefix over a floating static route and a static route that does not resolve, a recursive static route through a BGP prefix, a static route whose gateway is only under a BGP default route, the connected and OSPF routes that resolve beside the BGP prefixes, a static route whose next hop does not resolve, an OSPF version that is not computed in full and a next hop inside a longer BGP prefix.

`resolve.rs` is the one recursive next-hop resolution of all vendors: a collected path with a gateway and no interface resolves to the longest matching route of its VRF, through a default route too, and a route with a gateway recurses; an NX-OS next hop with a `%vrf` suffix resolves in that VRF; a VyOS route with a row with an interface forwards only on such rows, because FRR lists a recursive next hop before the next hops that resolve it; equal paths give one alternative for each resolved interface and gateway; a visited set detects a loop; a next hop with no route, a loop, or a route that is not read, unknown, local or a discard route gives `Unknown` with the reason `next hop not resolved: <next hop>`; the spans of each route of the resolution stay on the route. The flag `COMPUTED_THROUGH_DEFAULT` of each vendor profile applies only to computed routes.

`links.rs` holds the one link join of the estate: each device gives a summary (its ports with their entry functions, MACs and layer 3 addresses, the address, VLAN and VRF of each port address (`PortAddress`), its neighbour records, its MAC table and ARP rows with the VRF of each SVI in a VRF other than the default VRF, its next hops with the VRFs of their routes, its BGP peer facts `BgpPeerFact` and its local addresses of each VRF, loopbacks included, its collected routes for the source address of a BGP neighbour and whether its BGP config is read in full); the NX-OS switches, the VyOS and IOS-XR routers, the IOS-XE routers and switches, the root devices of each UniFi site and the VeloCloud edges give one, and the join gives the peers of each port, the place of each endpoint, the LLDP and CDP records that name a neighbour with no device of the twin (`Joined::seen`, kept in `Estate::seen`), and the far end `BgpFar` of each configured BGP neighbour with a peer address (`links::bgp_pairs`): 2 neighbours pair on the exact transport addresses, the far device holds the peer address in a VRF and has there a neighbour of the source address of the near side, else its dynamic peer, else the one listen range that holds it; the VRF names and the AS numbers are not part of the pairing, and the peer address of a neighbour whose peer is outside the twin is a next hop, so the join does not place it as an endpoint. The source address of a neighbour is the address of its update-source, else the one address of the family of the peer of the egress interfaces of the route lookup on the peer address through `resolve.rs` (`bgp_peers::lookup_sources`), else unknown with the reason `source address not resolved`. The BGP config of a device is read in full unless its coverage report or unread list has a BGP stanza that can hold a neighbour or a listen range (`estate::hides_peers`): a process, a VRF, an address family, or a neighbour, group, template or listen stanza itself; a setting line such as a password or a policy line below a neighbour holds none. The estate takes the collected routes out of each summary after the source lookup.

A device with no neighbour record and no MAC, such as a VeloCloud edge, has `Summary::by_address`, and the join links its ports by 2 address rules (`links::address_links`): `cable-from-arp-port`, an ARP row of another device for the address of such a port, whose MAC table rows lead, across the links of the neighbour records, to a switch port of the VLAN of the row, links that switch port; `bgp-peer-from-address`, the address that such a port names as its BGP neighbour or its gateway (`AddressPeer`), the untagged address of a port of another device, links that port. An address is evidence only when exactly one port of one device has it, in the same VRF when both sides state one, where the default VRF states none; a shared address (`Summary::shared_addresses`, an address after a NAT), an address of more than one port, an address in another VRF and an address on a VLAN subinterface give no link and a note. The placement leaves out each address of such a device. It knows no vendor, and `tests/links.rs` tests it on hand-built summaries. The tunnel facts of a summary join the VLANs with the same segment identifier on 2 tunnel ends that know each other, so the host table of a VLAN has the hosts behind the far tunnel end.

`mtu.rs` holds `InterfaceMtu`, the layer 3 MTU of an interface with the span of its value. `Estate::mtu` holds it by twin device and interface for the IOS-XR, IOS-XE, UniFi and VeloCloud devices. IOS-XE takes the MTU of `show interfaces` for the encapsulations of `iosxe::profile::MTU_ENCAPSULATIONS`. IOS-XR takes the MTU of `show interfaces` less the Ethernet header for `ARPA`, and less the header and one tag for `802.1Q Virtual LAN` with a VLAN (`iosxr::profile`). Each other encapsulation and an MTU of 0 give no MTU. A UniFi device with the boolean `jumboframe_enabled` gives each of its ports the frame of `unifi::lower::FRAME_BYTES` or `JUMBO_FRAME_BYTES` less 18 bytes, with the span of the device row. A VeloCloud WAN link gives the `MTU` of its WAN module row to its interface. A VeloCloud interface whose links give different MTUs has no MTU. Equinix gives no MTU.

`estate.rs` joins the routers, the UniFi sites and the NX-OS and IOS-XE switches into one twin; it lowers each NX-OS switch, each IOS-XR router and each IOS-XE device twice, first for its summary and then with its links, and an NX-OS or IOS-XE switch with its endpoints; `estate::assemble` takes the device inputs, so the tests in `tests/estate.rs` run on the synthetic sections in `crates/rsim-ingest/tests/fixtures/unifi/` with no store.
