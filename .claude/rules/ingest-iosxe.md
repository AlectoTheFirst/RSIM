# `rsim-ingest`, IOS-XE

The IOS-XE module is in `iosxe/`: `iosxe/table.rs` classifies the lines of the running config, `iosxe::read_text` puts the text lines of a banner below its `banner` line, `iosxe/mask.rs` masks the secrets with the mask of IOS-XR, `iosxe/config.rs` reads the interfaces, the VRFs, the named and numbered IPv4 ACLs, the static routes, NAT, the VLANs, the switch ports and the HSRP and VRRP groups with the readers of NX-OS, and a static NAT rule with `extendable` keeps that word as its uncertain word. `iosxe/state.rs` parses the text sections `interfaces`, `rib`, `arp`, `acls`, `lldp`, `cdp`, `hsrp` (`show standby brief`), `vrrp` (`show vrrp brief`), `hsrp_detail` (`show standby`), `vrrp_detail` (`show vrrp`, through the template of `show vrrp all`), `mac_table`, `spanning_tree`, `vlans` and `arp_vrfs` through the templates of `rsim-textfsm` into the row types of IOS-XR; each `VRF: <name>` part of `arp_vrfs` is parsed alone with the line numbers of the whole section, and an MST instance makes `spanning_tree` not read, because the section does not give its VLANs. In `show ip route vrf *`, the routes before the first `Routing Table: <vrf>` line are the global table, the VRF `default`; a table header after the routes of a table with no such line before it, or a VRF line of another form, starts a table whose VRF is not known, and each route of that table makes the lookup of its prefix unknown in each VRF.

`iosxe/lower.rs` lowers a router with the chain of NX-OS in the order of Cisco IOS: the input ACL, the session lookup, the outside NAT, the route lookup with the route table functions of `iosxr/lower.rs`, the inside NAT, the session commit, the next-hop resolution through ARP and the output ACL. A static NAT rule with an uncertain word gives match bounds for the packets that it can translate. A NAT role on an interface of another VRF and each unhandled line of an interface make the packets in and out of it unknown. The next hops of a VRF other than `default` go to the next-hop resolution when `arp_vrfs` gives the ARP table of that VRF, and else to `arp/<vrf>`, which gives `Unknown`: with no such section, with a line `VRF: <name> failed`, or with no part of that VRF. A switch port is not a layer 3 port. The summary of a router or a switch and the hand-off and the egress of a routed port with a link are those of IOS-XR, with a port channel member as its port channel. An HSRP or VRRP group is active, not active or unknown by the rule of IOS-XR from its row in `show standby` or `show vrrp` when the template reads that section, else in `show standby brief` or `show vrrp brief`. The virtual MAC is the MAC of `standby <group> mac-address`, else the virtual MAC of `show standby` or `show vrrp`; the brief forms give none, and an active group with no virtual MAC makes each frame of its port, or the hand-off of its SVI, unknown. A virtual MAC of the section that differs from the config, and an HSRP state that contradicts the active router of `show standby`, make the group unknown.

`iosxe/layer2.rs` gives the switch ports, the VLANs with VLAN 1, the SVIs and their gateway facts to the layer 2 lowering of NX-OS: a port with an address, a sub-interface or `no switchport` is routed, a switch port with no `switchport mode` line is unknown, with `vtp mode transparent` or `vtp mode off` the VLANs are those of the running config, else those of `vlans` (`show vlan brief`), where a VLAN that is not active makes each port that carries it unknown and a reserved VLAN with the status `act/unsup` is not a VLAN of the switch, and with no such section each switch port is unknown. With `spanning_tree`, a flood copy goes to a port only in a VLAN in which the port forwards (`FWD`); a port with no row or a state word that is not known in a VLAN of the flood goes to `port/<port>/stp`, which gives `Unknown`, except an edge access port with no row. With no such section, a flood copy toward a trunk or a port with an LLDP or CDP neighbour is unknown. `mac_table` gives the MAC lookup of each bridge domain and, with the ARP rows of the SVIs of the global table and of `arp_vrfs`, the endpoints on the edge access ports, as on NX-OS. With no MAC table, the MAC lookup knows the MACs of the switch and the MAC of the routed port at the far end of the link of a switch port with one peer (`External::far_macs`), a broadcast frame floods, and each other frame goes to `mac-miss`, which gives `Unknown`, and the switch has no endpoints. The `spanning_tree` state of each port reaches the layer 2 lowering of NX-OS as `nxos::state::State::stp`; NX-OS leaves it empty. `show ip access-lists` is a cross-check of the ACL entries and the static routes of the config are a cross-check of the route table; a difference gives a note. `iosxe/profile.rs` holds the vendor profile, the interface types for the short names and the words of the sections. The tests use the synthetic running configs of a router and of an access switch and their text sections in `crates/rsim-ingest/tests/fixtures/iosxe/`, with the prefix `switch-` for the sections of the switch; the configs have fake secrets.

`iosxe/bgp_config.rs` lowers the BGP config for the BGP calculation: the process of the default VRF and of each VRF of an `address-family <family> vrf <name>` stanza, with `bgp router-id`, where a VRF takes the line of its stanza, else the one of the global part, `bgp cluster-id`, `bgp always-compare-med`, `bgp deterministic-med`, `bgp bestpath compare-routerid`, `med missing-as-worst` and `as-path multipath-relax`, `bgp default local-preference`, `maximum-paths` and `maximum-paths ibgp`, `network` with a mask or the classful length and an optional `route-map`, `aggregate-address` with a mask or a length and `summary-only` and `as-set`, and the neighbours. A process with no router ID has a gap. The settings of a neighbour in a unicast family come from its `neighbor <peer> <keyword>` lines of the address family, and for IPv4 unicast of the default VRF also of the global part (`route-map <name> in` and `out`, `route-reflector-client`, `next-hop-self` and `next-hop-self all`, `send-community` with `standard`, `extended` or `both`, `remove-private-as`), then from the `template peer-policy` stanzas that it inherits by sequence number, then from its peer group. A neighbour of a VRF and each neighbour under `no bgp default ipv4-unicast` is active only in the families that its lines activate. The policies are the route maps, prefix lists, community lists and AS-path lists of the NX-OS reader (`nxos::bgp_config::policies`), with a numbered community list, standard from 1 to 99 and expanded from 100. Each import route target, `import map` and `export map` of a `vrf definition` or `ip vrf` is a gap of its VRF. `iosxe/bgp_config::collected` gives the BGP paths of the route table section.

## Commands

| Task | Command |
|---|---|
| IOS-XE ingestion tests | `cargo test -p rsim-ingest --test iosxe` |
| IOS-XE layer 3 tests | `cargo test -p rsim-ingest --test iosxe_l3` |
| IOS-XE link, HSRP, VRRP and Catalyst layer 2 tests | `cargo test -p rsim-ingest --test iosxe_links` |
| Coverage of an IOS-XE running config | `cargo run -p rsim-ingest --example iosxe_coverage < running-config.txt`. Each printed line goes through the mask of the secrets. |
